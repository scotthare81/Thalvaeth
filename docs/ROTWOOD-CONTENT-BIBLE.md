# Rotwood content bible

## Identity

Rotwood is the first extraction district and the proving ground for Thal'vaeth's systems. It must feel like an authored ruined place whose **population changes between runs**, not a procedural dungeon.

## Content rule

Every meaningful location should answer at least three questions:
1. Why would the player choose to go there?
2. What makes reaching/using it dangerous?
3. Which systems become interesting there?

Rewards should be ecological/functional, not merely a higher loot-table tier.

## Spatial structure

Author Rotwood as named segments/landmarks connected by readable routes, interiors, choke points and alternate paths. Exact final names/layout remain map-authoring work.

Each segment should carry:
- depth band;
- visibility/light profile;
- surface/noise profile;
- creature spawn-node families;
- resource ecology;
- traversal constraints;
- potential extraction/pressure hooks;
- setpiece eligibility.

## Landmark archetypes

Useful authored landmark roles include:
- ruined dwellings/cellars for scavenging and tight sound-driven encounters;
- workshop/industrial remains for metal/tool/process knowledge;
- tannery/butchery-like spaces for hides/rendering knowledge with cramped/noisy risk;
- wet/low ground for water/fishing/herb ecology and footing/visibility pressure;
- elevated catwalk/roof structures supporting Prowlers;
- fortified/choked spaces supporting Brute/Snare roles;
- Ash Hollows as scarce run recovery landmarks;
- deep corrupted spaces with higher threat/depth pressure.

These are design roles, not permission to invent final lore names without review.

## Creature ecology

90001–90010 are placed according to behaviour matrix and generation legality. Do not scatter every archetype everywhere.

Population variation should change route decisions while preserving location identity.

## Resources

Resource placement/ecology remains authored unless separately approved for procedural selection. Creature population randomness does not imply random loot.

Gathering opportunities should create exposure/time/noise decisions.

## Environmental storytelling

Use spaces, remains, tools, blocked routes, material traces and Journal discoveries to imply what happened. Avoid quest-marker exposition.

## Route design

Support:
- quiet safer-but-longer routes;
- exposed shortcuts;
- resource detours;
- interiors that trade sightlines for acoustic risk;
- retreat paths;
- loops/shortcuts that reward learned geography.

Because the map is stable, player mastery of geography is intended progression. Population randomness prevents complete encounter memorization.

## First content slice

The first playable Rotwood route must contain:
- one low-pressure approach;
- one meaningful fork;
- Scavenger/Drifter ordinary encounter possibility;
- one Sleeper teaching moment;
- one Prowler/vertical threat-capable space;
- one Brute/Snare-compatible choke or guarded resource;
- one Ash Hollow;
- one useful crafting/resource discovery;
- one extraction site;
- enough alternate nodes that two seeds visibly differ.

## Acceptance

Rotwood succeeds when the player learns the place while never knowing exactly what will occupy it this run.
